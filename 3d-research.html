Monza in 3D.
Evidence before confidence.

The new model uses geographic geometry, steering, combined grip, yaw response and 10 ms physics substeps. Its racing lines emerge from a minimum-curvature path and driver controllers. Public practice speed samples are used for comparison; they no longer prescribe vehicle speed.

This is a research prototype. The current model has larger unshifted speed errors than the previous telemetry-following version. Its track/timing datum, tire coefficients, aero maps and surface heights remain provisional. A better-looking world and passing consistency tests do not establish real-team engineering accuracy.

Model 6 · fixed wheel axles and progressive straight racing

The imported tyres contained baked camber. Fixed camber now sits outside axle spin, with cached corrected rims and concentric rounded slick envelopes. This addresses wheel precession rather than only smoothing steering. New races blend a longer open-straight steering preview; bends and wheel-to-wheel overlap retain close response. Old saves keep their original driving model. All 116 consistency tests pass, including 72 wheel/detail combinations through full revolutions and a completed side-by-side pass. In one paired 53-lap seed-2026 run all 12 cars finished: model 5 recorded 3 contacts/11 position-change events; model 6 recorded 1/4. This does not establish real-world accuracy or a guaranteed contact/overtaking rate. Evidence and limits.

The coarse imported front-flap group included nose and support triangles. Those pieces now remain fixed while isolated left/right control surfaces move on local hinges. 23 October · four-wheel road contact

Each tire now follows the shared road and pit surface independently, including authored kerb heights. A separate damped body follows slope/banking and responds to acceleration without lifting the wheels. Steering and axle spin have separate pivots; inner and outer wheels follow different turning radii. Cockpit and onboard cameras attach to the sprung body. Racing dynamics, resource accounting and save formats are unchanged. This is a presentation suspension and road constraint, not a validated vertical tire/suspension solver. Contact consistency evidence and supplied Monza asset audit.

What the tests establish

Deterministic commands and playback, save continuation, legacy-engine preservation, continuous track and pit connectivity, resource bounds, collision momentum, isolated-lap road containment, and controlled two-car braking scenarios. Paired analysis scenarios use fixed seeds and the same exogenous multipliers across candidate pit plans. Full analysis keeps the 100 Hz twelve-car dynamics; 200-scenario comparisons can take hours.

What remains assumed

Widths, banking, elevation, scenery, brake balance, tire-force scale and load sensitivity, aero coefficients, suspension response, and driver tendencies. Collisions use swept local-road rectangles and reduced-order impulses. Vertical vehicle motion follows the surface; there is no airborne rigid-body or detailed suspension solver.

FIA energy/aero rules retain the previous simplified frozen revision. Event-specific exceptions are not newly claimed. The braking model and public binary brake flag are different signals. Public data does not expose tire forces, brake pressure, steering angle, or complete battery/ERS maps.
