Detailed cars, transparent assumptions.

Six display meshes supplied by the user, adapted for browser racing. They improve the visual shapes; they are polygon models, not manufacturer CAD, a scan, or verified aerodynamic surfaces. No team or creator endorsement is implied.

Scale and mass

ChannelReference / implementation
WheelbaseF1 guide: maximum 3.4 m. All five adapted models normalized to 3.4 m; original axle measurements and transforms are retained in the manifest.
WidthF1 guide: 1.9 m floor width. A 1.9 m straight-wheel envelope is our visual normalization assumption, not a measured team dimension.
Minimum massF1 guide: 768 kg. New monza-3d-4 races use 768 kg plus remaining fuel as a simplified constant dry/driver mass. Old saved races without this parameter keep 770 kg.

F1 2026 beginner’s guide, checked October 22, 2026. Regulatory envelopes do not establish exact team dimensions or weigh-in details. Geometry changes alone do not reproduce real tire forces, downforce or drag.

High, Medium and far levels share immutable buffers across teammates. Wheels steer and rotate, flaps move automatically, and cockpit/T-cam anchors are fitted to the adapted model. The isolated-flaps-1 adapter now keeps nose, pylons and endplates rigid while conservative front/rear control-surface regions rotate separately. Hinge positions and travel remain illustrative; no manufacturer structural rig was supplied.

Grid and compatibility

New UI races use Ferrari (Leclerc/Hamilton), Mercedes (Russell/Antonelli), Red Bull (Verstappen/Hadjar), Audi (Hulkenberg/Bortoleto), Cadillac (Perez/Bottas), and McLaren (Norris/Piastri). Pairings: Formula 1. Twelve cars, six teams. Previous four/eight/twelve-car saves retain their grids. Existing teams lacking an imported model retain the original procedural model. New Audi/Cadillac driver traits are provisional and their car performance uses an unfitted neutral baseline, clearly distinct from equal mode normalizing every car.
